If you're thinking about pouring a new concrete driveway this year, the price tag is probably the first thing on your mind and honestly, it should be. A driveway isn't a small project. It's a few thousand dollars (sometimes more) sitting in your front yard for decades, so getting the numbers right before you sign a contract matters. Here's a real, practical breakdown of what homeowners in Nebraska are paying right now, plus the things that quietly push that number up or down.

So, What Does It Actually Cost?

Most Nebraska homeowners are landing somewhere between $6 and $12 per square foot for a concrete driveway. Translate that into a full project, and you're typically looking at $4,000 to $12,000 total, depending on how big the driveway is and how fancy you want it to look. Roughly speaking:

  • A basic, no-frills driveway: $6–$8 per sq. ft.
  • Something mid-range with a bit of finishing work: $8–$10 per sq. ft.
  • High-end decorative concrete: $10–$12+ per sq. ft.

The jump between those tiers usually comes down to reinforcement, how much site prep is needed, and whatever finishing techniques you choose.

Breaking Down the Per-Square-Foot Price

Thinking in price-per-square-foot is honestly the easiest way to budget, since it scales cleanly with whatever size driveway you're planning.

Standard Concrete (Broom Finish)

This is the plain-Jane option, functional, durable, and the cheapest way to get a solid driveway. Expect $6-$8 per sq. ft.

Reinforced Concrete

Nebraska's freeze-thaw winters are tough on concrete, so a lot of homeowners opt to add rebar or wire mesh for extra strength. That bumps the price to roughly $8-$10 per sq. ft., but it can save you money on repairs down the road.

Decorative Concrete

Stamped patterns, stained color, exposed aggregate, these finishes make a driveway look a lot more like a design feature than a slab of gray. Naturally, that look costs more: $10-$12+ per sq. ft.

What Actually Moves the Price Up or Down

A few variables tend to do most of the heavy lifting when it comes to your final bill.

Size and Shape of the Driveway

Bigger driveways usually cost a little less per square foot, but obviously more in total. And if your driveway curves, slopes, or has an odd layout, expect the labor cost to climb along with it.

Site Prep and Excavation

Got an old driveway to tear out first? Soil that needs grading or stabilizing? Nebraska's soil isn't uniform across the state, so prep work can vary a lot from one property to the next and it adds to the bottom line fast.

Thickness of the Slab

Most residential driveways are poured at 4 inches. If you're parking anything heavier trucks, RVs, trailers going to 5 or 6 inches adds durability, but also more material cost.

Labor

In Nebraska, labor generally eats up 40% to 60% of the total project cost, and rates shift depending on your area and who you hire.

Weather

Concrete and cold don't mix well. Nebraska's freeze-thaw cycles mean contractors have to time the pour carefully and sometimes take extra steps to protect the curing concrete, which can nudge the price up.

Don't Forget These Extra Costs

The per-square-foot number rarely tells the whole story. Here's what else can show up on the final invoice:

  • Tearing out an old driveway: $2-$4 per sq. ft.
  • Permits: Many Nebraska municipalities require one, typically running $50-$200.
  • Drainage: Proper slope and drainage planning costs a bit more upfront but saves you from cracking and water damage later.
  • Sealing: A protective sealant runs about $0.50-$1.50 per sq. ft. and goes a long way toward extending the driveway's life.

Concrete vs. Asphalt vs. Gravel

It helps to see concrete next to its main competitors:

Concrete - durable, low-maintenance, moderate cost upfront, but it pays off over the long haul.

Asphalt - cheaper to install initially, but it needs more upkeep and won't last nearly as long.

Gravel - the budget option, but it requires constant maintenance and just doesn't hold up the way concrete does.

For most homeowners weighing durability against long-term cost, concrete still comes out ahead.

A Few Ways to Keep Costs Down

You don't have to sacrifice quality to save money, you just have to be a little strategic:

  • Skip the decorative finishes if the budget is tight; a plain broom finish still looks clean and lasts just as long.
  • Try to schedule your pour during a contractor's slower season, pricing tends to be friendlier.
  • Get quotes from a few different contractors before committing. The spread can be bigger than you'd expect.
  • Be picky about add-ons. Not every upsell adds real value.
  • Don't skimp on base preparation, cutting corners here is the fastest way to end up paying for repairs later.

How Long Will It Actually Last?

A well-installed concrete driveway in Nebraska can hold up for 25 to 40 years. Keep it sealed and handle small cracks before they spread, and you'll likely get the longer end of that range. It's also worth noting that a clean, well-kept concrete driveway tends to boost curb appeal and resale value along with it.

Picking the Right Contractor

The lowest bid isn't always the smartest pick. Before you hire anyone, look for:

  • Real, demonstrable experience with driveway projects specifically
  • Familiarity with how Nebraska winters affect concrete
  • Pricing that's clear and itemized, not vague
  • Good reviews from actual past customers
  • Some kind of warranty or workmanship guarantee

Cutting corners on contractor selection is one of the easiest ways to end up with a driveway that cracks or settles early and that almost always costs more to fix than it would have to do right the first time.

Final Thoughts

Concrete driveway pricing in Nebraska in 2026 comes down to a handful of factors like size, finish, site conditions, and labor but the range generally falls between $6 and $12 per square foot. It's a real investment, no question. But given how long a properly poured driveway lasts and how little upkeep it needs compared to asphalt or gravel, most homeowners find it's money well spent.
