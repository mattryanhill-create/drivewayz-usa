Driveway Cold Pour Crack Filler: A DIY Guide to Repairing Asphalt Cracks

By the Drivewayz USA Editorial Team · Last Updated October 21, 2026

Small, stable cracks are a good DIY target when the label matches the crack, the surface is clean and dry, and you respect cure times.

A crack in your asphalt driveway can look simple but it's more than that. Water slips through the opening and soaks the layers underneath, and then the weather takes over. Temperature swings, freezing and thawing, and the weight of your car all work that gap wider. Before long you've got crumbling edges, and eventually a pothole. Cold pour crack filler is one of the easiest ways to stop that chain reaction while the cracks are still small.

It's a good fit for homeowners because there's nothing to heat and no special equipment to rent. Still, how well it works depends on three things: picking the right product, prepping the crack properly, and giving the filler enough time to cure.

What Is Cold Pour Crack Filler?

Cold pour filler is a liquid sealant you can use straight from the container, with no heating required. Depending on the brand, it might be built on asphalt emulsion, rubberized compounds, or a mix of other ingredients. These help it stick to the crack walls and flex a little as the pavement moves.

The alternative is hot-pour sealant, which needs a melter and some skill to handle safely. Cold pour products are friendlier. You can apply them with a squeeze bottle, a pour spout, or a basic applicator.

Also, remember it works best on isolated cracks in a driveway that's otherwise solid. Every product lists its own limits for crack width and depth, so read the label before you buy. Wide gaps, potholes, and driveways covered in interconnected cracks usually call for a different kind of repair.

Why It Is Important to Fix Cracks Early

Asphalt swells and shrinks as temperatures rise and fall. Once water gets into a crack, it can soften the base underneath, and in cold climates freezing makes everything worse. Drive over a weak spot often enough and it breaks down faster.

Sealing a crack keeps most of that water out, slows the damage, and makes the driveway look better. If the pavement underneath is still sound, it can also put off bigger repairs for a good while.

Example: Picture a thin crack running from your garage toward the street. Clean it and seal it and it's a quick weekend chore. Leave it through a few rainy seasons and the edges start to flake off, and the ground next to it may begin to sag. Filler won't stop every kind of pavement damage, but dealing with cracks early takes care of one of the biggest causes.

Choosing the Right Crack Filler

Driveway products aren't interchangeable, so it's worth getting this part right.

Match the Product to the Crack

Liquid cold pour filler is made for narrow to medium cracks, within the limits that manufacturer sets. Some products top out around half an inch, and others can handle more. If a crack is deep, you may need a backer rod or another approved filler to take up space at the bottom. That keeps you from using too much sealant and helps you control how deep the filler goes. For wider gaps, look at a trowel-grade patch or a proper asphalt repair. To compare crack filler products, read labels and reviews for your crack size.

Look at the Whole Driveway

Filler needs firm pavement around it. Alligator cracking, loose material, deep potholes, and obvious sinking won't be fixed by pouring sealant on top. A handful of separate cracks in an otherwise smooth surface is a fine DIY job. A section that looks like a pile of broken tiles probably means the base has failed, and you'll want a professional to take a look.

Steps to DIY

Gather everything first so you're not running to the store halfway through:

  • Cold pour crack filler that suits your crack size
  • A stiff wire brush or crack-cleaning tool
  • A screwdriver or scraper to dig out loose debris
  • A leaf blower or shop vacuum
  • An applicator, if the product calls for one
  • A putty knife or squeegee, if the instructions recommend it
  • Gloves and safety glasses

Keep kids, pets, and cars away while you work. Also make sure leftover filler and cleaning residue don't end up in a storm drain.

How to Apply Cold Pour Crack Filler

  1. Pick the right day: You want dry weather and a temperature inside the range printed on the product. Many fillers prefer mild conditions, but the exact numbers differ, so check the label. Look at the forecast too. Rain, damp pavement, or temperatures that are too hot or cold can ruin both the application and the cure.
  2. Clean the crack well: Pull out weeds, grass, dirt, loose bits of asphalt, and any old filler that's coming apart. Scrub the edges with a wire brush or crack tool, then blow or vacuum everything out. Dirt keeps the sealant from touching the asphalt, and it's the reason a lot of DIY repairs fail. Don't leave loose grit at the bottom. For more on cleaning, see asphalt crack repair DIY methods.
  3. Make sure it's dry: A surface that looks dry can still hold moisture deep in the crack. If you rinsed the driveway, give it plenty of time to dry out. Don't fill over damp asphalt unless the product says it's okay.
  4. Get the filler ready: Read the directions and apply it. Some products need a good shake and others don't. Don't thin it out or mix it with anything else unless the instructions say you can.
  5. Fill the crack: Pour or squeeze slowly, following the recommended depth and coverage. Work in short sections so the filler goes into the crack instead of spreading across the pavement. In a deep crack, don't dump it all in one. If the product settles or needs a second layer, follow the directions. One thick layer usually isn't the answer.
  6. Smooth it and let it cure: If the product calls for it, level the filler with a squeegee or another recommended tool. Try not to leave a raised ridge, since that can catch a snow shovel or rub against your tires. Then stay off it for the full time on the label. Curing depends on the product, how thick you applied it, the temperature, and the humidity. A surface that feels dry isn't necessarily ready for a car.

Mistakes That Ruin a Good Repair

Most failed DIY jobs come down to a few things. Filler goes over dust, weeds, or moisture and never bonds. The product isn't rated for that crack width. The weather was wrong. Or someone drove on it too soon.

The other big mistake is treating every crack as a simple sealing job. If the asphalt is soft, crumbling, sinking, or covered in cracks, figure out why before you fill anything. Sealant can't rebuild a failed base or replace pavement that's gone.

What Does Crack Filling Cost?

The cost depends on how much filler you need, the size of the cracks, the type of product, and whether you already own the tools. A few small cracks might take one container. A driveway with long, deep cracks can use far more.

Professional pricing varies too, based on where you live, how easy the driveway is to reach, how much prep is needed, and how bad the damage is. A contractor might suggest crack sealing, patching, or something bigger. If the damage looks widespread, get a quote based on a site visit before you decide whether DIY is worth it.

Final Thoughts

Cold pour crack filler is a practical way to look after an asphalt driveway, as long as the cracks are small and the pavement around them is stable. Clean prep, dry conditions, the right product, and a full cure make the biggest difference.
